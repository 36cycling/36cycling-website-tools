=== 1
<strong>Autumn is a beautiful season for cycling. The leaves turn yellow and red, temperatures are mild and the landscape is constantly changing. Yet this season also brings some challenges, such as cold, rain and shorter days. 36 Cycling gives a few practical tips to stay protected and comfortable on the bike in autumn.</strong>
=== 2
<strong>1. Choose the right layers</strong><br>In autumn the weather can change quickly. What starts as a crisp morning ride can end in a downpour. Wearing several layers of clothing helps you regulate your body temperature optimally. Start with breathable underwear that wicks away sweat and keeps your skin dry. The <a rel="noopener" href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/accessoires/fiets-onderkleding/" target="_blank" title="Base layers">base layers</a> from 36 Cycling keep your body temperature constant when it gets a little colder thanks to their insulating effect.

=== 9
When it comes to the limbs, some riders prefer to wear <a href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/accessoires/arm-en-beenstukken/" title="Arm and leg warmers">arm and leg warmers</a> when it gets colder and the days get shorter, because they give you the flexibility to take them (partly) off. You can also choose a water-repellent version. Others prefer to go straight for long tights (<a href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/heren/fietsbroeken/lange-fietsbroeken/" title="Men's bib tights">men's bib tights</a>/<a href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/dames/fietsbroeken/lange-fietsbroeken-dames/" title="Women's bib tights">women's bib tights</a>): with the bib tights from 36 Cycling you stay nice and warm on rides in less favourable weather. <br><br>Don't forget a buff or neck warmer to keep your neck warm and <a href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/accessoires/fietshandschoenen/" title="Cycling gloves">long gloves</a> for extra protection against the cold. We also often see riders wearing <a href="/nl/categorie/fietskleding/accessoires/wielerpetten-en-mutsen/" title="Cycling caps and hats">caps</a> under their helmets to protect their heads from the cold and to stop rain from getting behind their glasses and into their eyes.

=== 22
<strong>3. Make sure you are clearly visible</strong><br>The days are getting shorter and there's a good chance you'll be riding at dusk or even in the dark. Make sure you are clearly visible to drivers and other road users. This means always heading out with working front and rear lights, both in the dark and in bad weather. Choose clothing with reflective details, so you stand out at dusk too.
=== 23
<strong>4. Adapt your riding style to the weather</strong><br>In autumn, the road surface can be treacherously slippery due to wet leaves, mud and slick surfaces. Adjust your speed, especially in corners, and avoid sudden movements. Make sure your tyres have enough tread and check your tyre pressure regularly. A slightly lower tyre pressure gives more grip on wet roads.
=== 24
<strong>5. Hydration and nutrition</strong><br>Even though it's cooler, your body still loses fluid while cycling. So keep drinking enough, even if you are less thirsty than in summer. And don't forget to put snacks in your back pocket, such as energy bars or bananas, to keep your energy levels up on longer rides.
=== 25
<strong>6. Plan your route wisely</strong><br>In autumn, some roads are hard to ride because of mud or leaves. Choose routes with good roads and avoid paths covered in leaves. Check the weather forecast, use a wind app to see where you'll have a headwind or a tailwind, and preferably plan your ride in the warmer and drier parts of the day.
=== 26
With these tips you are well prepared to enjoy cycling in autumn to the full. Safety and comfort come first, so that you can hit the road without worries during this colourful season too.
